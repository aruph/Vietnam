Why this route in December

Vietnam runs three climates at once. In late December the north is cool and dry, the centre is still wet, and the south is at its sunniest.

Getting around, and the scooter question

Don't ride rental scooters on this trip

Vietnam only recognises International Driving Permits issued under the 1968 Vienna Convention. Canadian and Indian IDPs are issued under the 1949 Geneva Convention, so neither is valid there. Shops will still hand over keys, but police checkpoints in tourist areas now target foreigners, fines rose sharply in 2025, and travel insurance typically won't cover an unlicensed rider.

  • Ninh Binh: rent bicycles in Tam Coc (no licence needed). Flat paddy roads, perfect for the group.
  • Phu Quoc: book Grab or Xanh SM 7-seater electric cars; ride-hail motorbikes (with a driver) for 1–2 people.
  • Hanoi: walk the Old Quarter; Grab cars between districts.
  • Day trips: book a private 16- or 29-seat van with driver through your hotel for Ninh Binh and Lan Ha Bay.

Skin clinics in Hanoi

Hanoi has the stronger dermatology scene. Book for Day 1 afternoon, and go for a consult, hydrating facial or gentle treatment rather than lasers or peels: two beach days and snorkelling right after mean strong sun on freshly treated skin.

Eating vegetarian

Fish sauce hides in almost everything. Show these at street stalls:

Tôi ăn chay

"I eat vegetarian"

Không nước mắm

"No fish sauce"

Search "quán chay" on Google Maps for local vegetarian canteens, often near pagodas.

Prices, show times and opening hours change; confirm a week before you fly. Visas: every traveller needs Vietnam's e-visa (evisa.gov.vn), since Phu Quoc's visa-free rule only applies if you fly straight to the island and never leave it.
